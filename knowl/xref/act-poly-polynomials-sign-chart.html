Activity 5.2.3.

Consider the polynomial function given by
\begin{equation*} p(x) = 4692(x+1520)(x^2+10000)(x-3471)^2(x-9738)\text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

(a)

What is the degree of \(p\text{?}\) How can you tell without fully expanding the factored form of the function?
🔗
Solution.
The degree is \(1 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 6\text{.}\) We add the exponents from each factor: \((x+1520)^1(x^2+10000)^1(x-3471)^2(x-9738)^1\) contributes degrees \(1+2+2+1=6\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

What can you say about the sign of the factor \((x^2 + 10000)\text{?}\)
🔗
Solution.
The factor \((x^2 + 10000)\) is always positive for all real \(x\text{,}\) since \(x^2 \ge 0\) so \(x^2 + 10000 \ge 10000 \gt 0\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(c)

What are the zeros of the polynomial \(p\text{?}\)
🔗
Solution.
The real zeros are \(x = -1520\text{,}\) \(x = 3471\text{,}\) and \(x = 9738\text{.}\) (The factor \(x^2+10000\) has no real zeros since \(x^2 = -10000\) has no real solution.)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(d)

Construct a sign chart for \(p\) by using the zeros you identified in (c) and then analyzing the sign of each factor of \(p\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
The constant \(4692 \gt 0\text{,}\) and \((x^2+10000) \gt 0\) always, and \((x-3471)^2 \ge 0\) always (no sign change at \(x=3471\)). The sign is thus determined by \((x+1520)\) and \((x-9738)\text{:}\)
  • \(x \lt -1520\text{:}\) \((x+1520) \lt 0\) and \((x-9738) \lt 0\text{,}\) product is positive: \(p \gt 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • \(-1520 \lt x \lt 9738\) (and \(x \ne 3471\)): \((x+1520) \gt 0\) and \((x-9738) \lt 0\text{:}\) \(p \lt 0\text{.}\) (At \(x=3471\text{,}\) \(p=0\) and the sign does not change.)
    🔗
    🔗
  • \(x \gt 9738\text{:}\) both factors positive: \(p \gt 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗
🔗

(e)

Without using a graphing utility, construct an approximate graph of \(p\) that has the zeros of \(p\) carefully labeled on the \(x\)-axis.
🔗
Solution.
The graph is positive (above the \(x\)-axis) for \(x \lt -1520\text{,}\) crosses zero at \(x=-1520\) (passes through), touches zero at \(x=3471\) (bounce, even multiplicity), remains negative until \(x=9738\text{,}\) then crosses zero and becomes positive. The leading term \(4692 x^6\) means both ends go to \(+\infty\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(f)

Use a graphing utility to check your earlier work. What is challenging or misleading when using technology to graph \(p\text{?}\)
🔗
Solution.
Using a graphing utility, the zeros at \(x=-1520\text{,}\) \(3471\text{,}\) and \(9738\) are spread over a large \(x\)-range, making it challenging to see all features at once. It is difficult to tell from the graph that \(x=3471\) is a bounce point (touching zero without crossing) unless the scale is set appropriately.
