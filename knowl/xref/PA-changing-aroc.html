Preview Activity 1.3.1.

Let the height function for a ball tossed vertically be given by \(s(t) = 64 - 16(t-1)^2\text{,}\) where \(t\) is measured in seconds and \(s\) is measured in feet above the ground.
🔗
🔗

(a)

Compute the value of \(AV_{[1.5,2.5]}\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
First compute \(s(1.5) = 64 - 16(1.5-1)^2 = 60\) and \(s(2.5) = 64 - 16(2.5-1)^2 = 28\text{.}\) Then
\begin{align*} AV_{[1.5,2.5]} &= \frac{s(2.5)-s(1.5)}{2.5-1.5} = \frac{28-60}{1} = -32. \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

What are the units on the quantity \(AV_{[1.5,2.5]}\text{?}\) What is the meaning of this number in the context of the rising/falling ball?
🔗
Solution.
The units are feet per second. The value \(AV_{[1.5,2.5]} = -32\) means that between \(t = 1.5\) and \(t = 2.5\) seconds, the ball’s height decreases at an average rate of \(32\) feet per second.
🔗
🔗
🔗

In Desmos, plot the function \(s(t) = 64 - 16(t-1)^2\) along with the points \((1.5,s(1.5))\) and \((2.5, s(2.5))\text{.}\) Make a copy of your plot on the axes in the figure provided, labeling key points as well as the scale on your axes. What is the domain of the model? The range? Why?

Solution.
Without additional context specifying when the ball is launched or lands, the domain and range of the model are not fully determined by the equation alone. The formula \(s(t) = 64 - 16(t-1)^2\) is a classical free-fall equation, and negative values of \(t\) or \(s\) are not necessarily unphysical.

🔗

(d)

Work by hand to find the equation of the line through the points \((1.5,s(1.5))\) and \((2.5, s(2.5))\text{.}\) Write the line in the form \(y = mt + b\) and plot the line in Desmos, as well as on the axes above.
🔗
Solution.
The slope is \(m = AV_{[1.5,2.5]} = -32\text{.}\) Using the point \((1.5, 60)\) to find the vertical intercept:
\begin{align*} 60 &= -32(1.5) + b\\ b &= 108. \end{align*}
The equation of the line is \(y = -32t + 108\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(e)

What is a geometric interpretation of the value \(AV_{[1.5,2.5]}\) in light of your work in the preceding questions?
🔗
Solution.
\(AV_{[1.5,2.5]}\) is the slope of the secant line connecting the points \((1.5, s(1.5))\) and \((2.5, s(2.5))\) on the graph of \(s\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(f)

How do your answers in the preceding questions change if we instead consider the interval \([0.25, 0.75]\text{?}\) \([0.5, 1.5]\text{?}\) \([1,3]\text{?}\)
🔗
Solution.
Interval \([0.25, 0.75]\text{:}\) \(s(0.25) = 55\text{,}\) \(s(0.75) = 63\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} AV_{[0.25,0.75]} = \frac{63-55}{0.5} = 16 \text{ ft/sec.} \end{equation*}
The secant line through \((0.25, 55)\) with slope \(16\) is \(y = 16t + 51\text{.}\)
🔗
Interval \([0.5, 1.5]\text{:}\) \(s(0.5) = 60\) and \(s(1.5) = 60\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} AV_{[0.5,1.5]} = \frac{60-60}{1} = 0 \text{ ft/sec.} \end{equation*}
The secant line is the horizontal line \(y = 60\text{.}\)
🔗
Interval \([1, 3]\text{:}\) \(s(1) = 64\text{,}\) \(s(3) = 0\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} AV_{[1,3]} = \frac{0-64}{2} = -32 \text{ ft/sec.} \end{equation*}
The secant line through \((3, 0)\) with slope \(-32\) is \(y = -32t + 96\text{.}\)
