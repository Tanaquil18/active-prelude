Activity 1.2.2.

Consider a spherical tank of radius \(4\) m that is filling with water. Let \(V\) be the volume of water in the tank (in cubic meters) at a given time, and \(h\) the depth of the water (in meters) at the same time. It can be shown using calculus that \(V\) is a function of \(h\) according to the rule
\begin{equation*} V = f(h) = \frac{\pi}{3} h^2(12-h)\text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

(a)

What values of \(h\) make sense to consider in the context of this function? What values of \(V\) make sense in the same context?
🔗
Solution.
Since depth can’t be negative and can’t exceed the tank’s diameter of \(8\) m, the values of \(h\) that make sense are \(0 \le h \le 8\text{.}\) The corresponding values of \(V\) range from \(0\) (empty tank) to \(f(8) = \frac{256\pi}{3}\) (full tank), so \(0 \le V \le \frac{256\pi}{3}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

What is the domain of the function \(f\) in the context of the spherical tank? Why? What is the corresponding codomain? Why?
🔗
Solution.
The domain of \(f\) in context is \([0, 8]\text{,}\) since the water depth can range from \(0\) m (empty) to \(8\) m (full diameter). The range is \(\left[0, \frac{256\pi}{3}\right]\text{.}\) Since volumes are non-negative, the codomain can be taken as \([0, \infty)\text{,}\) or more broadly as all real numbers if we consider the abstract formula without physical constraints.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(c)

Determine and interpret (with appropriate units) the values \(f(2)\text{,}\) \(f(4)\text{,}\) and \(f(8)\text{.}\) What is important about the value of \(f(8)\text{?}\)
🔗
Solution.
\begin{align*} f(2) &= \frac{\pi}{3}(4)(10) = \frac{40\pi}{3} \approx 41.9 \text{ m}^3\\ f(4) &= \frac{\pi}{3}(16)(8) = \frac{128\pi}{3} \approx 134.0 \text{ m}^3\\ f(8) &= \frac{\pi}{3}(64)(4) = \frac{256\pi}{3} \approx 268.1 \text{ m}^3 \end{align*}
These give the volume of water when the depth is \(2\text{,}\) \(4\text{,}\) and \(8\) meters respectively. The value \(f(8) = \frac{256\pi}{3}\) equals the volume of a sphere of radius \(4\text{,}\) confirming that the tank is completely full when \(h = 8\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(d)

Consider the claim: “since \(f(9) = \frac{\pi}{3} 9^2(12-9) = 81\pi \approx 254.47\text{,}\) when the water is \(9\) meters deep, there is about \(254.47\) cubic meters of water in the tank”. Is this claim valid? Why or why not? Further, does it make sense to observe that “\(f(13) = -\frac{169\pi}{3}\)”? Why or why not?
🔗
Solution.
Neither claim is valid. The domain of \(f\) is \([0, 8]\text{,}\) so \(h = 9\) is outside the domain — the water cannot be \(9\) m deep in a tank whose diameter is only \(8\) m. Similarly, \(f(13)\) falls outside the domain, and the negative value it produces is further evidence that the formula should not be applied beyond \(h = 8\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(e)

Can you determine a value of \(h\) for which \(f(h) = 300\) cubic meters? Why or why not?
🔗
Solution.
No. The maximum value of \(f\) on its domain \([0, 8]\) is \(f(8) = \frac{256\pi}{3} \approx 268.1\) m\(^3\text{,}\) which is less than \(300\text{.}\) The tank simply cannot hold \(300\) cubic meters of water.
