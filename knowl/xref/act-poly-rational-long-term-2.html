Activity 5.4.3.

Let \(s(x) = \frac{3x - 5}{7x^2 + 2x - 11}\) and \(u(x) = \frac{3x^2 - 5x + 1}{7x + 2}\text{.}\) Note that both the numerator and denominator of each of these rational functions increases without bound as \(x \to \infty\text{,}\) and in addition that \(x^2\) is the highest order term present in each of \(s\) and \(u\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(a)

Using a similar algebraic approach to our work in Activity 5.4.2, multiply \(s(x)\) by \(1 = \frac{\frac{1}{x^2}}{\frac{1}{x^2}}\) and hence evaluate
\begin{equation*} \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3x - 5}{7x^2 + 2x - 11}\text{.} \end{equation*}
What value do you find?
🔗
Solution.
Multiplying \(s(x) = \dfrac{3x-5}{7x^2+2x-11}\) by \(\dfrac{1/x^2}{1/x^2}\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} s(x) = \frac{\frac{3}{x} - \frac{5}{x^2}}{7 + \frac{2}{x} - \frac{11}{x^2}}. \end{equation*}
As \(x \to \infty\text{,}\) the numerator \(\to 0\) and the denominator \(\to 7\text{,}\) so \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty} s(x) = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

Plot the function \(y = s(x)\) on the interval \([-10,10]\text{.}\) What is the graphical meaning of the limit you found in (a)?
🔗
Solution.
The graph of \(y = s(x)\) has a horizontal asymptote at \(y = 0\text{.}\) Far to the left and right, \(s(x)\) approaches the \(x\)-axis. This occurs because the denominator grows much faster than the numerator (degree 2 vs. degree 1).
🔗
🔗
🔗

(c)

Next, use appropriate algebraic work to consider \(u(x)\) and evaluate
\begin{equation*} \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3x^2 - 5x + 1}{7x + 2}\text{.} \end{equation*}
What do you find?
🔗
Solution.
Multiplying \(u(x) = \dfrac{3x^2-5x+1}{7x+2}\) by \(\dfrac{1/x^2}{1/x^2}\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} u(x) = \frac{3 - \frac{5}{x} + \frac{1}{x^2}}{\frac{7}{x} + \frac{2}{x^2}}. \end{equation*}
As \(x \to \infty\text{,}\) the numerator \(\to 3\) and the denominator \(\to 0^+\text{,}\) so \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty} u(x) = +\infty\text{.}\) The numerator degree exceeds the denominator degree, so there is no horizontal asymptote.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(d)

Plot the function \(y = u(x)\) on the interval \([-10,10]\text{.}\) What is the graphical meaning of the limit you computed in (c)?
🔗
Solution.
The graph of \(y = u(x)\) increases without bound as \(x \to \infty\text{;}\) \(u\) has no horizontal asymptote. Instead, it has an oblique (slant) asymptote. The graph shows that \(u(x)\) grows like a linear function for large \(|x|\text{.}\)
